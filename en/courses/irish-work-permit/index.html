Why this is hard

The problem isn't the red tape. It's the wrong information.

You have already searched. You found the immigration website, you found the employment department website, you found a group on Facebook, you asked an AI. And you got four different answers.

It isn't just you. The official Irish websites contradict each other, many pages are out of date, and nobody tells you which one counts. Looking for clear, straight information there is maddening.

The result is people making expensive decisions on free information.

  • People who came as tourists thinking they would sort it out here, and found out they have to leave the country.
  • People who let their permission expire and now have to explain six months of silence to immigration.
  • People who turned up at immigration with an address different from the one they had declared, and left with a letter telling them to go.

What changes in practice

The difference between understanding and hoping

Those who hope

People who don't understand the process turn up at the interview hoping. They accept the first salary offered, without knowing there is a legal floor. They find out too late that the role sits on a list that ruled the application out from the start. And they end up depending on someone else to translate every email that arrives.

Those who understand

People who understand turn up differently. They know which permit to apply for and why. They know there is a minimum salary for each route and they use it in the conversation. They know whether the job they are looking at can turn into a permit before investing time in it. And they can talk the process through with an employer without handing over all of their own uncertainty.

This isn't a detail. The moment the company sees that you understand the path, you stop being a piece of red tape and become a candidate.

Before you decide

Frequently asked questions

Are a work permit and a visa the same thing?

No. They are two documents, issued by two different authorities. The work permit comes from the employment department and allows a company to hire you. The visa, the Stamp 1, is the residence card immigration issues afterwards. One depends on the other, and the course starts precisely with that difference.

Do I need a job offer already to take the course?

No. Plenty of students take the course precisely to know what to look for before they start applying.

Does the course teach me to apply on my own?

No, and that is deliberate. The course shows how the process works, who does each part, and what decides your eligibility. The application itself is filed by the company or by a responsible agent.

I'm in Brazil. Is it for me?

It is. Most of the process happens from the outside in: you need to arrive in Ireland with the document in hand. The course covers that scenario and also the one of people already here.

I already live in Ireland on a student visa. Is it for me?

It is, and it is one of the most frequent cases. The course covers moving from Stamp 2 to Stamp 1 without leaving the country.

I work in a field that doesn't look 'critical'. Is it worth it?

It is, and it is one of the misunderstandings the course clears up. A lot of people think Critical Skill is only for board-level roles. Depending on the company and the salary, the same role can qualify.

Is the information still valid?

The rules change, and the course says so openly. Lesson 10 is entirely given over to showing where to check official information and how to spot an out-of-date page — so that you don't depend on any course, this one included.
